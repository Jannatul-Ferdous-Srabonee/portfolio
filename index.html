I am a PhD candidate in Electrical Engineering and Computer Science at the University of California, Merced, where I conduct research in the Inclusive Interaction Lab advised by Dr. Ahmed Arif. I completed my BSc in Computer Science and Engineering from Khulna University of Engineering & Technology, Bangladesh.

My research focuses on designing and developing inclusive input and interaction techniques for digital systems. My current work spans gesture-based techniques, eye-tracking, applied artificial intelligence (AI), and brain-computer interfaces (BCIs), particularly for individuals with motor and vision impairments. Beyond these, I actively look to other domains for inspiration and translate what I find into new tools in pursuit of more inclusive interaction.

One of my earlier research projects on the development of an EEG‑based depression screening system, received national recognition with the BASIS National ICT Winner Award. Alongside this, my work has been published and accepted at premier Human-Computer Interaction venues including ACM CHI and MobileHCI, as well as in Scopus-indexed peer‑reviewed journals.

  • Exploring the Impacts of HEXACO Personality Traits on Text Composition and Transcription

    Jannatul Ferdous Srabonee, Ohoud Mosa Alharbi, Wolfgang Stuerzlinger, & Ahmed Arif.
    — ACM CHI 2025

    What if your writing style could tell a system about your personality? This study examines how personality traits shape text entry
    behavior across composition and transcription tasks. By analyzing speed, accuracy, editing effort, and readability, it reveals
    distinct trait‑specific influences on creative versus mechanical writing.

  • EEG Based Autism Detection Using CNN Through Correlation Based Transformation of Channels' Data

    Zahrul Jannat Peya, MAH Akhand, Jannatul Ferdous Srabonee, & Nazmul Siddique.
    — IEEE TENSYMP 2020

    Conventional autism screening depends on questionnaire‑based assessments and multidisciplinary clinical evaluations, processes that
    are time‑consuming and often yield preliminary insights rather than definitive diagnoses. In contrast, this study demonstrates that
    autism spectrum disorder can be identified directly from EEG signals, providing a rapid, objective approach.

    Conference Paper PDF

    Zahrul Jannat Peya, MAH Akhand, Jannatul Ferdous Srabonee, & Nazmul Siddique.

  • Alcoholism Detection from 2D Transformed EEG Signal

    Jannatul Ferdous Srabonee, Zahrul Jannat Peya, MAH Akhand, & Nazmul Siddique.
    — IJCACI Springer 2020

    Traditional alcoholism screening methods often lack diagnostic precision, while blood alcohol content testing, though more objective, requires
    additional time and clinical resources. This work demonstrates that alcoholism can be detected directly from EEG signals alone,
    offering a faster, non-invasive alternative that achieves 98% classification accuracy.
